Filament colours Set these to the filaments you will load. Every pixel takes the nearest of these colours. They start as the picture's own colours.
Dark at the bottom usually looks best: light filament on top covers dark cleanly. Each colour is printed on top of the one below, so only colours that sit above the first need a filament change.
Size
Use a multiple of your layer height. 0.2 mm covers the colour below cleanly with most filaments. Thinner (0.08-0.12 mm) lets the lower colour show through for blended, glowing effects, as in tools like HueForge.
Printing tips
  • Orientation: print flat, exactly as exported, with no supports. The picture is on the top surface.
  • 3MF (Orca / Bambu / PrusaSlicer): one part per colour, already assigned to filament slots 1, 2, 3... from the bottom up. Load your filaments into those slots. A multi-colour printer (AMS, MMU, toolchanger) prints it as is.
  • One extruder: use the STL and add a filament change at each height listed above, in your slicer's layer slider. The colour that goes on first is the bottom colour in the list.
  • Layer height: make the base and every step a whole number of layers. For example 0.2 mm layers with a 0.8 mm base and 0.2 mm steps gives 4 layers, then 1 layer per colour.
  • Details: with a 0.4 mm nozzle, anything narrower than about 0.4 mm will not print. Fewer colours and more smoothing give cleaner results. Wide, simple shapes work best.
  • Frame it: add a border by making a rectangular picture, or print the piece and mount it in a frame.
