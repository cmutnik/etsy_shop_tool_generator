What to make
Size (in Gridfinity units)
One unit is 42 mm across (the bin is 41.5 mm so it clears its neighbour) and 7 mm tall. The height includes the stacking lip.
A baseplate is 42 mm a cell, so a 220 mm bed takes 5 cells each way. Print bigger floors as several plates side by side.
Compartments
A scoop is a curved ramp that makes small parts easy to pick out. 8-12 mm suits a 2-unit-deep bin.
Features
Printing tips
  • Orientation: print as exported, foot on the bed, open top up, no supports. The lip's underside is a 45 degree slope and the magnet pockets are short bridges.
  • Settings: 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls (the 1.2 mm wall is exactly three lines), 10-15% infill for the solid floor. Print the first bin alone and test it in a baseplate before you print a batch.
  • Fit: the foot follows the Gridfinity standard (41.5 mm, stepped), so it drops into any standard baseplate. Printers differ by a few hundredths of a millimetre, so if it is tight, calibrate your flow and horizontal expansion; do not change the model.
  • Stacking lip: built from the foot's own profile with 0.25 mm of clearance and a printable underside. It is not copied from the official files, so check one stacks on another before you sell a set.
  • Magnets: 6 x 2 mm discs, four per grid cell, in the bin's feet and in the baseplate pockets. Glue them in all the same way round (check with a spare). Keep neodymium magnets away from small children, pacemakers and bank cards.
  • Baseplates: print flat, top up. A baseplate pocket is the same shape as the stacking lip, so bins fit by the same numbers. Print one cell first and drop a bin in it: the foot should sit down with a little play and no wobble. Glue the plate to a board or use double-sided tape so it does not slide.
  • Selling it: Gridfinity is an open system and its dimensions are free to use. Check the licence of any bin or baseplate files you did not make yourself. To print a set, send several bins to the Plate Nester.
