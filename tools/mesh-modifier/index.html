Model 3MF files carry their own units, so leave this on millimetres for them. For an STL, pick what the model was designed in. You can also drag a file onto the preview.
    Untick a part to leave it out, or change its colour. The last box is its filament slot for multi-colour printing: leave it on auto and parts of the same colour share a slot, or type a number to choose. Slots read from a 3MF are kept. Colours and slots go into the 3MF.
    Size and orientation
    Scaling is along the printer's X, Y, Z after rotating. Turn off "Keep proportions" to stretch one direction. Tabs and labels are placed on the model as shown here.
    A round hole in the direction you pick, measured from the model's centre (and from the bed for height). Depth 0 goes all the way through; otherwise it is measured from the end you drill from. Needs a closed (watertight) model. Keep at least 2 mm of wall around a hole you hang something from; a hanging tab is stronger at an edge.
    Cut after the size and rotation above. The upper half is dropped onto the bed so both pieces print flat on their cut faces. The pegs are a separate part on the lower half; the sockets are cut into the upper half. Needs a closed (watertight) model.
    The tab lies flat on the bed, so it prints without supports, and it is a separate part that overlaps the model so the slicer fuses them. A positive move goes clockwise seen from above. Hole edge to model must stay clear, so keep the wall at least 0.8 mm more than "sinks in".
    Text goes on the highest surface under it. Put it on a flat area; you will see a warning if the surface is uneven or the text hangs past the edge. Engraving needs a closed (watertight) model and leaves at least 0.8 mm of floor. "Fill the engraving" adds the lettering as its own part, exactly filling the pocket, for a two-colour print: use the 3MF download (it keeps the parts and filament slots; an STL merges the two shells).

    Notes
    • Everything stays in your browser. The file is read on this page and never uploaded.
    • 3MF keeps its parts and colours. Each object in a 3MF stays its own part with its name and colour, and a mesh painted in several colours becomes one part per colour. Added tabs and text are extra parts, so a 3MF download keeps them separate for multi-colour printing. An STL has no colours, so everything is merged into one shell.
    • What a 3MF loses: slicer project settings (print profiles, filament assignments, support painting) are not read. Re-assign filaments in your slicer after opening the result.
    • Check the model first: the line under the preview reports open edges. A model that is not watertight may slice badly, and cannot be cut. The Repair button merges duplicate points, removes bad triangles, fixes inside-out faces and fills small holes; it only says a part is fixed when it really is closed afterwards, and you can undo it. It cannot fix large missing areas or self-intersecting models. A part that was inside-out (its faces pointing inwards) is turned the right way round automatically, and you are told.
    • Lay flat turns the model so its largest flat face rests on the bed, which is usually the best print orientation for a model that was saved on its side.
    • Cuts (hole, engraving, cut in two) use a 3D geometry engine (manifold-3d, about 0.5 MB) that is only downloaded the first time you turn one on. They need every part to be a closed solid; the page tells you which part is not.
    • Hanging tabs are added at the model's outline at bed level, so the model should sit on the bed (the default) and have a flat bottom at that edge.
    • Results are always in millimetres, Z up, on the bed at Z = 0.
