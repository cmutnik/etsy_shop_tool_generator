Model 3MF files carry their own units, so leave this on millimetres for them. For an STL, pick what the model was designed in. You can also drag a file onto the preview.
    Size and orientation
    Scaling is along the printer's X, Y, Z after rotating. Turn off "Keep proportions" to stretch one direction. Tabs and labels are placed on the model as shown here.
    The tab lies flat on the bed, so it prints without supports, and it is a separate part that overlaps the model so the slicer fuses them. A positive move goes clockwise seen from above. Hole edge to model must stay clear, so keep the wall at least 0.8 mm more than "sinks in".
    Text is added to the highest surface under it. Put it on a flat area; you will see a warning if the surface is uneven or the text hangs past the edge.
    Open a file to start.
    Notes
    • Everything stays in your browser. The file is read on this page and never uploaded.
    • 3MF keeps its parts and colours. Each object in a 3MF stays its own part with its name and colour, and a mesh painted in several colours becomes one part per colour. Added tabs and text are extra parts, so a 3MF download keeps them separate for multi-colour printing. An STL has no colours, so everything is merged into one shell.
    • What a 3MF loses: slicer project settings (print profiles, filament assignments, support painting) are not read. Re-assign filaments in your slicer after opening the result.
    • Check the model first: the line under the preview reports open edges. A model that is not watertight may slice badly, and this tool does not repair it.
    • Hanging tabs are added at the model's outline at bed level, so the model should sit on the bed (the default) and have a flat bottom at that edge.
    • Results are always in millimetres, Z up, on the bed at Z = 0.
